# A9 — optimization accessibility of exact low-byte conditional functions

Date: 2026-11-15. Design fixed **before A9 training and audit**. Status: exploratory follow-up of A8/A8b; not a preregistered independent confirmation of general Mirror capacity.

## Question
A8 proved that shear-Mirror and pre-shear variants represent all 16 ramp functions exactly in a 244-byte FP32 serialized model, but random-initialized training failed to reach the quality gate. Is this due to the representation family or an optimization/initialization barrier? Is an oracle-free canonical scaffold easier to train with only task codes than the same model from random initialization, and is the effect Mirror-specific?

## Worlds, data, model, storage
- A9 main worlds **820–825** (6), seeds **0,1**, function **ramp** only. A9 smoke world **819** is never included in final results. World-generation algorithm and train/dev/IID/OOD splits are unchanged from A8: 16 tasks, 1024/384/384/384 shared inputs respectively, x train/dev/IID uniform [-1.5,1.5] and OOD uniform [-2,2]; labels are closed-form ramp teacher functions, no Mirror used in teacher.
- Variants `mirror`, `pre`, `shift`, each with exactly 4 hidden neurons for five training regimes (`random_full4`, `random_code4`, `scaffold_full4`, `scaffold_code4`, `scaffold_noisy4`) and `random_wide` with the greatest allowed width under 512-byte FP32 weight-file cap. **216 runs** (6 worlds × 2 seeds × 3 variants × 6 regimes).
- The canonical scaffold encodes two ReLU hinges with shared first-layer features, and per-task 2-scalar codes. Shared scaffold construction is independent of each world's teacher thresholds and right-endpoint labels. Codes initialize uniformly in [-0.4,0.4] from a method-independent seed; they are NOT initialized to teacher thresholds.
- `scaffold_code4` freezes the shared network, trains only the 32 task-code scalars. `random_code4` freezes a random shared network with the same trainable codes. The other arms update all parameters. `scaffold_noisy4` adds mean-zero Gaussian noise (SD 0.1) to the scaffold **shared weights/biases only** before training; noise independent of teacher labels. The canonical/noisy scaffold is an unusually strong **family-aware** inductive bias; this is a diagnostic, not a general model-selection comparison.
- `mirror` and `pre` exactly coincide on the fixed canonical scaffold because the second ReLU outputs in each shear pair are identically zero; `shift` admits an equivalent direct code-shift realization. Their serialized weights use the existing A8 format: 32B header, all FP32 parameter tensors in declared order, 32B SHA256 trailer. At hidden width 4, `mirror/pre` each use 244B and `shift` uses 276B. Decoder executable code and teacher-generator program are not priced. All quantities dimensionless.

## Fixed training/audit
- PyTorch CPU FP32, one numerical thread per process, deterministic algorithms, AdamW lr=0.012, weight decay 0, 1200 updates, batch 256, gradient norm clipping 5, seed-specific frozen task/x minibatch stream. At most 4 parallel processes. All final-step weights serialized and SHA256 locked **before** any dev/IID/OOD evaluation. No intermediate stopping or validation-driven hyperparameters.
- Record post-lock train/dev/IID/OOD MSE and task-variation-normalized RMSE. The quality gate is IID and OOD NRMSE <= 0.08. Compare **paired world/seed** under the same width4 and steps, as well as to each same-architecture `random_wide` reference. Code-only and full-network regimes are not matched in trainable degrees of freedom; this is the variable being tested. Report per-world mean and empirical count across six worlds. Do not call one family of worlds multiple independent real-world datasets.
- Pre-registered diagnostic H1: `scaffold_code4` produces joint gate successes in at least 5/6 worlds for `mirror`, and has lower paired IID MSE than `random_full4` in at least 5/6 world means. H2: the canonical code-only Mirror behaves **numerically equivalently** to `pre` and `shift` (up to FP32 rounding), so any gain from the scaffold is **not Mirror-specific**. Under fixed scaffold, equivalence is algebraic; under full training, models can diverge.
- PASS H1 only if both world gates are met; FAIL if either is not. H2 algebraic PASS requires maximum pointwise abs discrepancy < 1e-6 and task-code gradient discrepancy < 1e-5 at init on 16 test tasks and 64 x points. If H1 fails, record negative result, do not change protocol. General compression advantage remains UNCERTAIN unless matched-quality/bytes comparisons pass separately.
- Additional diagnostics: initial task-code gradient norm and fraction of features on active ReLU branch for each method, stable filenames/weights for bitwise audit and <=3 independent replay runs.

## Caveats, uncertainty, falsification
- C: scaffold advantage might be entirely due to injecting the known functional form of the teacher; it does not indicate a Mirror-specific capacity advantage. Extra update steps may favor one optimizer parameterization; matched training FLOPs are not claimed.
- U: finite train draws, 2 seeds/world, limited 6 worlds from the same synthetic family, FP32 rounding, non-convex optimization, fixed initialization and optimizer, model/source description bytes unpriced. Independent-world bootstrap or exploratory t intervals may be reported, but n=6 is too small for broad general claims.
- The A8 strict family-aware construction and direct task-threshold codec (192B for ramp, incl 64B header/trailer) remain stronger upper-bound references than trained neural models.

### Exact aggregation rules (locked before smoke and main training)
For each world/variant/regime, report mean of two seed-level MSEs and **mean of two seed-level NRMSEs** separately on IID and OOD. A world passes the joint gate iff both mean NRMSE values are <=0.08. Compare `scaffold_code4` against `random_full4` by mean paired IID MSE per world. Log ratios are geometric averaged across the six worlds with a descriptive Student-t 95% interval (worlds, not models, are the independent units). No p-value significance claim from the post-A8 exploratory family.
