# MA-393 — Adaptive-capacity embedding plus token Mirror view

Status: protocol frozen before development. Dedicated branch: `research/ma-393-adaptive-capacity-mirror-embedding-20261009`.

## Mirror insertion

**Mirror insertion:** add a token-specific angle after frequency-band projection to recover one functional degree of freedom without widening every rare-token embedding.

## H — Hypothesis

A per-token Mirror angle restores useful variation in low-capacity frequency bands, especially for rare tokens, at complete payload bytes no greater than a simple adaptive embedding with one extra coordinate per token.

## T — Frozen design

PA60 motivates adaptive vocabulary capacity. Use 384 tokens, three frequency bands with latent widths 16/8/4, and a fixed seeded projection into 16 dimensions. Compare full embeddings, standard adaptive widths, a byte-matched 17/9/5 adaptive control, and adaptive codes plus a token Givens angle. Train with band masses .70/.22/.08 and report frequency-weighted and per-band quality. See `PROTOCOL.json` for frozen gates and seeds.

This deliberately aligned synthetic orbit tests representation feasibility. It is not a real Zipf corpus or language-model result.

## D — Result

**Fact.** In corrected development worlds 39301/39302, frequency-weighted embedding NRMSE was .00301/.00407 for Mirror, .00374/.00247 full, .150/.145 standard adaptive, and .128/.133 for the byte-matched +1-latent-coordinate control. Rare-band NRMSE was .0124/.0154 for Mirror versus .5368/.4418 adaptive and .5363/.4081 byte control. Mirror weighted decoder top-1 was 100.00%/99.96%. Actual payload was 15,427/15,404B versus full 25,512/25,491B (60.46%/60.43%, narrowly above the frozen ≤60% gate) and byte control 15,289/15,254B. All eight corrected payloads replayed bytes, hashes and metrics; four tests pass. Fresh 39311–39313 stayed sealed.

**Interpretation.** The Givens coordinate recovered the teacher's rare-token output variation at essentially the same payload as an ordinary extra latent coordinate, where the byte-matched adaptive control remained far worse. It narrowly missed the strict complete-payload saving target against full embeddings. The Mirror operation also had higher cost: training took 2.04/2.12s versus .48/.47s for full and about 1.05s for adaptive controls; inference throughput was 1.44/1.70M tokens/s versus ~13.3M for full and ~2.7M for adaptive. This is a quality/bytes learning-efficiency signal on an aligned fixture, not a capacity claim.

**Hypothesis.** Rare-token variation in this teacher lies on a cheap rotational orbit that a fixed narrow projection cannot express, while a generic added latent scalar cannot reproduce the rotated subspace. The storage miss is caused by charging one angle for each token plus fixed projection metadata; no natural-data benefit is established.

## H / T / D / C / U

- **H:** Per-token angles recover useful rare-token function in adaptive 16/8/4 bands at no more than the bytes of adding one ordinary coordinate/token.
- **T:** PA60; 384 synthetic tokens, frequency masses .70/.22/.08, fixed seeded projections and classifier, full/adaptive/byte-matched/Mirror controls, two development seeds, 2,000 Adam updates, complete compressed payloads.
- **D:** **FAIL on the frozen full-payload gate, with a strong aligned mechanism result.** Mirror crushed both adaptive controls in rare-band NRMSE and nearly matched full quality, but was 60.4% of full payload versus the ≤60% limit. Fresh stayed sealed.
- **C:** The task teacher was explicitly generated by the same rotation family, and Mirror paid additional training and inference compute; a learned natural embedding may not share this orbit.
- **U:** Fresh seeds, natural Zipf text, learned projection bases, deployment latency on hardware accelerators, and whether a narrower/private-residual frontier retains rare-token gains.

## Correctness amendment record

An initial development probe used separately generated projections for the standard and +1-coordinate controls, so the latter did not preserve the original adaptive subspace. Its raw outputs and payloads are retained under `results/excluded_pre_correction_non_nested_byte_control/` and excluded from all claims. The projection was corrected to nest the original basis, tested, committed before the corrected development rerun, and no fresh seed was accessed.
