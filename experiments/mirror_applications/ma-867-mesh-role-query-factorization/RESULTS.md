# MA-867 results

## Fact

- Synthetic policy world: eight roles × eight queries; true log precision was generated by a rank-2 role/query factor product. 75% of pairs were observed for fitting and 25% held out. The policy selected two roles per query.
- Fresh seeds 601, 617, 631 had heldout utility MAE 0.0000, 0.0002, 0.0000 for the rank-2 fit.
- Mean query normalized MSE:
  - Factorized rank-2: .2802, .2705, .2430.
  - Flat observed lookup: .2897, .2786, .2668.
  - Full-information oracle: .2802, .2705, .2430.
  - Random roles: .3256, .3204, .3058.
- Actual policy payload: 136 B for two 8×2 factor tables, versus 271 B for the full 8×8 table plus observed-pair mask. Across eight queries, factorized mode sent 16 messages / 96 B; all-role central mode sent 64 / 384 B. One-hop latency for both.
- Native rank-2 matrix factorization and the Mirror factor decoder produce the same role scores and selections by construction. The factorized policy used 600 fitting updates and 128 scoring MACs.

## Interpretation

In this low-rank synthetic setting, factorization recovers unseen role-query pairs, modestly improves over a flat table, matches the oracle, and halves policy bytes. It also cuts the message budget by 4×. These results support native low-rank role/query factorization for this artificial world.

Mirror-specific attribution fails: the same rank-2 matrix factorization is the native control and generates identical scores, selections, and MSE. The gain comes from the low-rank factor structure, not an additional Mirror operation.

## H / T / D / C / U

- **H:** A factorized role × query address can generalize to unseen pairings while reducing policy bytes and messages.
- **T:** Eight roles and queries, rank-2 latent utility, 75/25 train/heldout pair split, 600 Adam updates, top-2 selection, fresh worlds 601/617/631; flat lookup, full oracle, and random controls. Actual serialized payload, communication, compute, and latency measured.
- **D:** **FAIL** for Mirror-specific attribution; the native rank-2 factorization is functionally identical. The native factorized system passes this synthetic screen with MAE ≤.0002 and 136 B vs 271 B flat policy.
- **C:** The world was generated exactly by a rank-2 factor model, so the low-rank method has a favorable specification match; natural Mesh utility may be higher rank or nonlinear.
- **U:** Real agent utilities, noisy/incomplete role descriptors, non-low-rank worlds, network topology, and privacy leakage remain untested.

This is a synthetic collective-inference policy result, not neural-network compression evidence.
